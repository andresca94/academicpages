Workflow deep dive

Barbara Feldon AI Character Lab

This case study uses Barbara Feldon as a controlled character system for testing restoration, identity-consistent generation, virtual styling, 3D translation, lip-sync, face swap, and image-to-video motion inside a single creative workflow.

  • Restoration and inpainting
  • LoRA character consistency
  • Virtual try-on and 3D tests
  • Lip-sync, face swap, and motion output

Workflow arc

  1. Collect high-signal portraits and clean them for training quality.
  2. Use FLUX-based restoration, inpainting, and upscaling to stabilize source material.
  3. Train a LoRA to preserve identity across stylized scenes and mood shifts.
  4. Extend the character system into fashion, 3D, and motion-oriented experiments.
  5. Package outputs that feel usable for concept films, avatar tests, and creative direction.

01 · Input set

Source imagery selected for identity coverage

The workflow started with a curated set of Barbara Feldon portraits chosen for clarity, lighting variety, and representative facial angles. Those images became the basis for restoration, LoRA training, and later motion experiments.

02 · Identity + refinement

Restoration, upscaling, and LoRA-based consistency

FLUX was used to repair, extend, and sharpen the source material before training character consistency. The goal was not just cleaner images, but a stronger identity prior that could survive style shifts, lighting changes, and later video passes.

03 · Fashion + spatial extensions

Virtual styling and dimensional translation

Once the character system stabilized, the workflow moved into fashion-adjacent and spatial experiments. That included apparel visualization, broader stylization, and 2D-to-3D conversion to see how the same identity could extend into richer creative outputs.

04 · Motion outputs

Lip-sync, face transfer, and image-to-video movement

The final stage focused on performance and motion: face swap modules, lip-sync portrait animation, and image-to-video tests that turned the static character system into something closer to an edit-ready creative asset.

05 · Why this workflow matters

A repeatable lab for AI-native character development

Creative value

This was a compact R&D environment for testing how one character could survive multiple AI modalities: cleanup, style transfer, wardrobe experimentation, dimensional conversion, and motion.

Tooling value

It also served as a systems exercise in chaining together FLUX, LoRA workflows, try-on tooling, and motion modules into a process that could support concept development rather than isolated outputs.
